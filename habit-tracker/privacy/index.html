Privacy Policy

Habit Tracker · Effective 22 November 2026

In short

Your habits, check-ins and memos stay on your iPhone or iPad. There is no account and no sign-in. Habit Tracker shows no ads, does not track you across other companies' apps and websites, and does not sell your data.

The app does send some data: crash reports, basic usage analytics, and the error reports, messages and feature requests you choose to send. Each is explained below.

What stays on your device

Your habits. Habit names, goals, check-ins, skipped days, memos and mood emoji, streaks and medals are stored only on your device. We never receive them.

iCloud Sync, only if you turn it on. It is off until you turn it on in Settings → Backup & Sync. Then your habits and logs are also kept in your own private iCloud database through Apple's CloudKit, so your iPhone and iPad show the same data. That copy belongs to your Apple Account, and we can't see it.

Apple Health. If you turn on Apple Health Sync for a habit, the app reads only the kind of data you pick for it: steps, walking and running distance, cycling distance, swimming distance, water, exercise minutes, mindful minutes or sleep. It reads the last 7 days and uses each day's total to fill in that habit's progress. Habit Tracker never writes anything to Apple Health. Health data is never sent to us or anyone else, never used for advertising or marketing, and never sold. The amounts it fills in become part of your habit log on the device, and in your private iCloud if you turned on iCloud Sync.

Screen Time. For a habit that Screen Time counts, you choose apps with Apple's Screen Time picker. Apple gives Habit Tracker only private tokens for them, not your app or browsing history. The app's Screen Time extensions learn only when you reach the minutes you set, so they can send a reminder, show the pause screen or celebrate a goal. This stays on your device. It is never sent to us or anyone else and never used for advertising.

Reminders. Habit reminders and Screen Time reminders are notifications scheduled on your device. So is the automatic "we've got your report" notice after you send a report.

App Lock. Unlocking uses Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode through iOS. The app only learns whether unlocking worked. It never sees your face data, fingerprint or passcode.

Backups and spreadsheets. Exporting makes a backup file or a spreadsheet that you save or share wherever you choose. Importing reads a backup file you pick. We never receive these files.

Share Progress. The picture of your progress is made on the device. It goes only where you send it, or to Photos if you choose Save Image. The app can add pictures to Photos but can't see your library.

The app's log. The app keeps a log of what it did, such as "opened Settings" or "a save failed", on the device (up to 5 MB, left out of device backups). The log never contains what you write: habit names, memos and messages are left out, and habits are named only by a short code. It leaves your device only as described below.

Your random ID

Habit Tracker has no accounts. Instead, the first time it needs one, the app creates a random ID and keeps it in your device's Keychain. It is not based on your name, email, phone number, Apple Account or device, and it is not the advertising identifier.

The ID stays on this device and is not synced through iCloud Keychain. It stays even if you delete the app, so after reinstalling you can still see the reports you sent. It goes with your crash reports, analytics and messages, so we can match them up, for example to find the crash behind a report you sent. Your messages can only be read with this ID, so it is never shown on the public Feature Requests board.

What leaves your device

Crash reports and diagnostics (Google Firebase Crashlytics). If the app crashes, a crash report is sent with technical details of the crash, the device model, iOS version, app version, the most recent part of the app's log and your random ID. If something fails without a crash, such as a save, a short description of the error is sent the same way. The log rules above still apply: no habit names, memos or messages.

Usage analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase). Analytics records standard events such as the first time the app is opened, sessions and how long they last, screens viewed, and app and iOS updates. Each event comes with the device model, iOS version, app version, language, an approximate location (country or city) that Google works out from your IP address, your random ID and an installation ID that Firebase creates. Habit Tracker uses the version of Firebase Analytics that does not collect the advertising identifier (IDFA), so the app never asks to track you, and doesn't.

Update checks (Google Firebase Remote Config). The app asks Firebase from time to time whether a newer version is recommended or required.

Error reports, only when you send one. In Settings → Help & About → Report an Error you choose the type of problem and describe it, and you can add a screenshot. Taking a screenshot or shaking the device only offers to start a report, with a picture of the app's screen. Nothing is sent until you tap Send. A report contains what you write, the screenshot if you added one, and the app's log.

Messages, only when you send one. A Contact Us message contains what you write and the app's log. Each report has a chat, where we can reply and you can answer.

Feature requests and likes, only when you send them. The title and description of a feature request are shown to everyone who uses Habit Tracker, with the date and its number of likes and dislikes, so please don't include personal information. Who sent a request is stored separately and is not shown to anyone else. Likes and dislikes are stored as a one-way fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) of your random ID, which can't be turned back into the ID or used to reach your messages.

Sent with each report, message and feature request (once, not with every chat message): your random ID, the app version, device model, iOS version, and the region and language set on your device. Reports, messages and feature requests are stored in Google Cloud Firestore; screenshots and logs in Cloud Storage for Firebase.

Links to other services. Rating the app uses Apple's rating prompt, and Follow opens LinkedIn. Sharing uses the share sheet, so what you share goes only where you choose. Those services have their own privacy policies.

Service providers

These companies process data for Habit Tracker under their own privacy policies:

  • Google Firebase for crash reports, analytics, update checks, and the reports, messages and feature requests you send
  • Apple for iCloud Sync, Apple Health, Screen Time and notifications, which stay within your Apple devices and your Apple Account

Google stores the reports, messages and feature requests in India, and screenshots and logs in the United States. Analytics and crash data may be processed in the United States and other countries.

Habit Tracker does not sell your personal data, does not share it with data brokers, and does not use it for advertising.

Your choices

  • Apple Health, notifications and Screen Time are only asked for when you turn them on for a habit. You can change access any time in the Settings app.
  • iCloud Sync is off unless you turn it on in Habit Tracker → Settings → Backup & Sync.
  • Delete All Data in Habit Tracker → Settings → Backup & Sync removes every habit and log from the device, and from iCloud if iCloud Sync is on.
  • Error reports, messages and feature requests are only sent if you choose to.

Keeping and deleting data

Your habits stay on your device, and in your iCloud if you use iCloud Sync, until you delete them.

Error reports, messages, screenshots and logs are kept until they are no longer needed to answer you, fix problems or plan the app, or until you ask for them to be deleted. Feature requests stay on the board until we take them off it. Analytics and crash data are kept according to Google Firebase's retention settings and then deleted automatically.

To have your data deleted, send a message from Settings → Help & About → Contact Us asking for it. Because the message carries your random ID, we can find and delete everything you sent, and ask Firebase to delete the analytics data linked to the same ID. You can also email us. Because there are no accounts, please describe what you sent and roughly when.

Children

Habit Tracker is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you think a child has sent us information, contact us and we will delete it.
